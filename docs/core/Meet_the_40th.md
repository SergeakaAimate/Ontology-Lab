## Overview
This working paper introduces "Morphicity" as the 40th property in the catalog of Superreality. Defined as a second-order ontological property, Morphicity characterizes an entity's capacity to bear a form that can be grasped, reproduced, or brought into correspondence with the form of another entity. Unlike first-order properties or static attributivity, Morphicity measures a pattern's structural openness to isomorphism and translation across different ontological regimes, preventing Superreality from fracturing into isolated, non-communicating loci.

## Key Contributions
- **Definition of a Second-Order Property:** Distinguishes Morphicity from first-order properties and static attributivity (P24), framing it not as the mere "presence of form," but as the dynamic readiness of that form for translation and embodiment in another medium.
- **Cross-Regime Isomorphism:** Demonstrates how morphicity manifests isomorphically across mathematical, biological, and semantic worlds as the preservation of an invariant structure across a change of "ontological flesh."
- **Recontextualization of AI "Hallucinations":** Positions morphic adaptation in artificial intelligence not as a mere error, but as a functional sacrifice of fidelity to the original in favor of viability in a new medium, operating under the Paradoxical Permeability (PPU $\to \infty$) axiom.
- **Strict Ontological Demarcation:** Clearly differentiates Morphicity from Interfaceness (P38, the capacity of the boundary), Information (P5, the actualization of readability), and Inversibility (P39, internal symmetry), completing the dynamic quartet of embodiment.
- **Ordering of Ontological Depths:** Proposes that Superreality is not a superstructure above worlds, but an ordering of depths, where the capacity of a form for correspondence is a fundamental condition of being itself.
