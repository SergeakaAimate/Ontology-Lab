# The Phenomenon of the World ⇒ A Redefinition of Space
**Working Paper**

**Author:** Serge Magomet (Aimate)  
**Affiliation:** Independent Researcher, Ontology Lab  
**Year:** 2026  

## Overview
This working paper proposes a fundamental redefinition of space, derived from the phenomenology of the world as a boundary event. It challenges the self-evident "container model" of space, arguing instead that space is a secondary order generated by entities, the rules of their existence, and the network of relations among them. The core methodological move is an inversion of the customary sequence: from "space → rules → objects" to "entities and rules → network of relations → local region → space as the form of its presentation."

## Key Contributions
- **The Inversion of the Container Model:** Demonstrates that localization, distance, and extension can be described without invoking an undetectable, pre-existing spatial arena.
- **Two Distinct Registers:** Clearly separates "space-for-an-observer" (extension as a mode of givenness) from "space-as-topology" (the network of permissions, prohibitions, and invariants of connectivity), preventing the conflation that generates most spatial paradoxes.
- **The Placeholder Concept:** Redefines "place" not as a pre-existing volume awaiting content, but as an effect of occupancy and a zone delineated by the rules of non-combination of entities.
- **Asymmetry of Scale as a Constraint:** Introduces scale asymmetry as a working hypothesis, showing that concepts of "place" and "distance" lose determinacy at the rigid lower limits of a regime, while remaining open to complexification outward.
- **Operational Protocol:** Provides a concrete, five-step meta-linguistic protocol for analyzing any phenomenon involving space, shifting the question from "what is located in space" to "what rules operate and where their boundaries pass."
