# The Phenomenon of the World

**Author:** Serge Magomet (Aimate)  
**Project:** Ontology Lab  
**Document Type:** Working Paper  
**License:** CC BY-NC  

---

## 📄 Overview
This working paper develops a line of inquiry shared with speculative realism and object-oriented ontology (OOO)—specifically, the rejection of correlationism and the irreducibility of worlds. However, it shifts the ontological focus from *objects* to *regimes of fixation* and *boundaries*. 

Where OOO places the autonomous, withdrawn object at the center, this text argues that objects are constituted in fixation; irreducibility is a property of regimes, not of objects; and boundaries are internal conditions of determinacy rather than external limits.

## 🔑 Core Contributions
1. **Inter-world Interaction:** Supplies a concrete mechanism for how worlds interact—not through "vicarious causation," but through *double fixation*, where a single operation acquires determinacy in two regimes simultaneously.
2. **Structural Criterion:** Provides a clear distinction between a "world" (an ontological regime) and a local system of rules operating within that regime.
3. **Self-Applicability:** The text about the world is itself a distinction conducted from within, making it subject to the same limits and "blind spots" it describes (a structural Gödel-likeness).

## 🧠 Key Concepts
- **Regimes of Fixation:** The conditions (rules, medium, boundaries) under which something acquires determinacy.
- **Asymmetry of Scale:** Movement "inward" (toward foundations) encounters rigid limits, while movement "outward" (toward generalization) remains open.
- **The Blind Spot:** Not a gap in knowledge, but the structural inability of a regime to formulate certain questions from within its own rules.
- **Double Fixation:** The mechanism by which one operation is translated and acquires determinacy across different ontological regimes (e.g., physical, semantic, phenomenal, normative).
