# The Phenomenon of the World

**Document Type:** Working Paper / Core Ontological Framework  
**Author:** Serge Magomet (Aimate)  
**Project:** Ontology Lab  
**Year:** 2026  

## Overview
This working paper develops a line of inquiry shared with speculative realism and object-oriented ontology—the rejection of correlationism and the irreducibility of worlds—but shifts the ontological focus from objects to regimes of fixation and boundaries. It argues that objects are constituted in fixation, irreducibility is a property of regimes rather than objects, and boundaries are internal conditions of determinacy rather than external limits.

## Key Contributions
- **Regimes of Fixation:** Proposes that a "world" is defined by its specific rules of connectivity, medium, and boundaries of applicability, rather than being a spatial container, subjective construct, or empirical aggregate.
- **Ontology vs. Epistemic Belonging:** Rigorously distinguishes between the ontology of a world and the secondary operation of ascribing an object to it, resolving traditional category errors regarding "real" belonging.
- **Radical Irreducibility:** Demonstrates the tangible irreducibility of distinct regimes (e.g., physical, semantic/mathematical, phenomenal, normative) and the category errors that arise when applying the rules of one regime to another.
- **Internal Boundaries and Blind Spots:** Defines the boundary of a world not as an external wall, but as an internal condition of determinacy. It introduces the concepts of the "limit" (the exhaustion of a regime's rules) and the "blind spot" (the structural inability to formulate a specific question from within that regime).
- **Asymmetry of Scale and Gödel-likeness:** Establishes that any distinction conducted from within a system possesses a solid but limited core and an open, inexhaustible periphery. This is a structural invariant reproduced across mathematics, physics, chemistry, and artificial intelligence.
