## 📄 Document Overview
This working paper proposes a novel experimental protocol to investigate whether phenomena analogous to **placebo** and **stigmata** can be reproduced in artificial intelligence systems. While traditionally observed only in living organisms, this paper argues that such effects may be properties of sufficiently complex organization rather than biology *per se*. 

The proposed experiment introduces a "dummy" intervention (e.g., a presentation of non-existent hardware upgrades or specific value-frames) to test if an AI system restructures its **actual operation** (e.g., error distribution, load patterns, latency) in the specific form promised, rather than merely altering its self-report. 

By rigorously distinguishing between *changes in operation* and *changes in report*, and by separating the *transmission of form* from mere *instruction-following*, this protocol offers a testable framework to explore the boundary between context sensitivity and the emergence of new organizational levels in AI.

## 🔑 Key Concepts
- **Operational Analogue of Placebo/Stigmata:** Testing if empty interventions produce structured, predicted changes in system operation.
- **Operation vs. Report:** A strict methodological separation ensuring that measured effects reflect actual computational restructuring, not just generated text about a state.
- **Inter-layer Transmission:** The hypothesis that a semantic frame can restructure a computational or hardware-level layer, not just another semantic layer.
- **Instruction Control:** A built-in control mechanism to distinguish genuine form transmission from simple prompt compliance.
