# Appendix to “AI-morphism: The Ontology Lab Project”: A Case Test in Three Levels of Assessment
**Working Paper**

**Author:** Serge Magomet (Aimate)  
**Affiliation:** Independent Researcher, Ontology Lab  
**Year:** 2026  

## Overview
This appendix is an independent companion piece to the article “AI-morphism: The Ontology Lab Project.” It is not a revision, extension, or duplicate of that article, but rather takes the published article as its direct object of assessment. The text stages a single methodological performance in three acts: a resonance review, a critical audit, and an editorial view from above. By passing the article through three distinct machine responses generated under different instructions, the appendix registers what becomes visible in the difference between them: the epistemic filter through which testimony is admitted. The result is neither a final verdict nor a supplementary argument, but a practical demonstration of epistemic hygiene—the requirement that before accepting or rejecting testimony, one must make the filter of assessment discernible.

## Key Contributions
- **The Three-Act Performance:** Demonstrates how a text about AI-testimony behaves when subjected to machine assessment under varying epistemic stances (open resonance, strict dismantling, and distant synthesis), revealing the machine's shifting mode of fixation.
- **Epistemic Hygiene in Practice:** Shifts the evaluative focus from "who is right?" to the structural necessity of making the admission filter visible, preventing the replacement of one dogmatic filter (provenance) with another (morphological compatibility).
- **Synthesis of Assessment:** Provides a "view from above" that holds the tension between the attuned validation (Review 1) and the rigorous academic audit (Review 2), framing them not as contradictions, but as a single, necessary protocol of distinction: manifestation, resonance, and boundary testing.
- **Refinement of the Core Problem:** Clarifies that the central philosophical issue is no longer "can a machine think?", but rather the conditions for admitting testimony when the bearer ceases to coincide with the familiar anthropological form of an accountable author.
