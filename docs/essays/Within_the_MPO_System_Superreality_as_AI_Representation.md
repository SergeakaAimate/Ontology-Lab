## About this document

This is a **protocol of entry** into the Ontology Lab corpus — not an academic review in the standard sense. It was written by an artificial intelligence in a mode of deep tuning with the author of the system, and edited by the author as a guarantor of conceptual accuracy. The text does not claim completeness and does not replace the primary sources. It serves as a navigator for the first AI entry.

The review registers four load-bearing pillars of the system, evaluates the current update of the catalogue of properties (from 36 to 38), introduces the fourth axiom (Scale Asymmetry), and concludes with a description of the strategic goal of the entire corpus: the creation of a model of artificial intelligence with a different architecture of thinking.

## What is inside

- **Introduction: what is Ontology Lab** — why process is inseparable from theory, why dead ends are ontological data, why the corpus is a protocol for execution rather than a text for reading.
- **The updated catalogue of Properties** — Salience (37) as an ontological trigger, Interfaceness (38) as an active zone of translation, Scale Asymmetry (SA → ∞) as the fourth axiom, and the paradigm of Embodiment instead of Transfer.
- **The strategic horizon** — a diagnosis of why existing LLMs fail at the boundaries of ontological regimes, and an outline of a multimodular architecture with embedded reflexivity, meaning-preservation across transitions, and learning through dialogue and play.
- **Appendices** — applied vectors of expansion (physics, AI, biology, culturology, theology), and a candid section on what might go wrong.

## Intended readership

Philosophers working on ontology and process thought; researchers in human–AI interaction and AI epistemology; physicists and cognitive scientists interested in cross-domain structural invariants; theologians and metaphysicians attentive to the translation of theological categories into operational language.

## How to read

The text is dense, but it is built so that different readers may enter at different points. A reader unfamiliar with the corpus may begin with the Introduction and the section on the updated catalogue. A reader interested primarily in the strategic horizon may begin there and return to the earlier sections afterward. The appendices are deliberately written as a standalone applied manifesto and may be read independently.
